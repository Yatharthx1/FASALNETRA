# Choose crop.health, then add free data

FasalNetra should add a **crop.health key, not a plant.id key**. crop.health is Kindwise's food-crop model and lists soybean (*Glycine max*) among its 23 supported crops. plant.health is trained mostly on houseplants and ornamentals ([Kindwise crop.health](https://www.kindwise.com/crop-health); [Kindwise plant.health](https://www.kindwise.com/plant-health)). The key is self-serve, and new accounts get **100 free credits**. After that, calls cost **€0.05 each** at the 1,000-credit tier ([Kindwise pricing](https://www.kindwise.com/pricing)). Expect less than the vendor's validation scores. Accuracy falls from **85% top-1 on validation to 48% top-1 on independent Twitter photos** ([Kindwise blog](https://www.kindwise.com/post/crop-health-curing-crops-empowering-farmers)), and there is no evidence that it has a class for **yellow mosaic virus (YMV) or girdle beetle**. Treat it as a top-3 shortlist that leads into the app's "shak" (suspected) flow, not as the diagnosis. On the prediction side, the biggest gains need no new key at all. The first is the soil-moisture, VPD, ET and leaf-wetness variables, plus ensemble forecasts, that Open-Meteo already serves. The second is Sentinel-1 radar from the same Planetary Computer endpoint the app already signs against. Within a day, a team can also add observed rainfall (IMERG via a free NASA Earthdata login, or IMD gridded data with no key), iNaturalist presence records, district soybean area, and KCC query history from data.gov.in. KCC history is the weakest link to obtain. The data.gov.in resource page reportedly offers no API, so the team will probably have to download a CSV. The sources that would truly calibrate a model are the national pest-surveillance app NPSS, Maharashtra's CROPSAP scout data, and ICAR's KCC-CHAKSHU archive. All three need institutional requests and belong on the production roadmap. Until then, published numeric thresholds from Madhya Pradesh (MP) and nearby states are the best calibration inputs, and Section 5 lists them.

## crop.health fits soybean fields, but probe it before trusting it

The two Kindwise products are separate services with separate keys. crop.health runs at `crop.kindwise.com`. It has **about 288 classes across 23 crops**: about 180 pests, 80 fungal, 20 viral, 20 bacterial and 2 other ([Kindwise crop.health](https://www.kindwise.com/crop-health)). plant.health (the plant.id health assessment) has 548 classes. Kindwise says outright that "most annotations are for houseplants and ornamentals", and the expanded model scores **58% top-1 / 73% top-3** ([plant.health page](https://www.kindwise.com/plant-health); [Kindwise expansion post](https://www.kindwise.com/post/plant-health-major-expansion-from-90-to-500-diseases)). plant.health also does not identify the crop, and a full health call costs 2 credits. Buying both keys would double the spend for no gain.

Coverage of the seven target problems is the open question. Kindwise publishes no per-crop class list. The public demo collection contains no soybean images, but its outputs show how coarse the classes are ([crop.health demo collection](https://crop.kindwise.com/collection/x8X1VTfxUshyS7z)):

| FasalNetra target | Likely crop.health output | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Whitefly (*Bemisia*) | **Aleyrodidae** (family) | Seen in demo; soybean context unverified |
| *Spodoptera litura*, semilooper | **Noctuidae / Erebidae / Geometridae** (family) | Seen in demo; no species-level Spodoptera class seen |
| Rhizoctonia aerial blight | ***Rhizoctonia solani*** | Class exists (seen on other crops) |
| Anthracnose | ***Colletotrichum*** | Class exists (seen on other crops) |
| Soybean rust (*Phakopsora pachyrhizi*) | Unknown | **Unverified**; only maize rust seen |
| YMV (begomovirus) | Unknown | **Unverified and high risk**: only about 20 viral classes across all crops, and no mosaic class was seen |
| Girdle beetle (*Obereopsis brevis*) | Probably none | **Unverified**; India-specific damage that is hard to see in photos |

The practical consequence is that the existing Kindwise adapter should map the **stable suggestion `id`**, not the `name`, onto rows of the ICAR advisory table. Family-level caterpillar hits should go to a generic "defoliating caterpillar" advisory. YMV and girdle beetle should keep coming from the app's own vision path and symptom questions.

**How to get the key.** First, sign up through the Kindwise admin panel. The examples README still links the legacy `admin.mlapi.ai/signup`, and the current panel is probably `admin.kindwise.com` (**unverified**) ([crop-health-examples](https://github.com/flowerchecker/crop-health-examples)). Choose crop.health when you create the key. Second, check the key's limits and remaining credits with `GET https://crop.kindwise.com/api/v1/usage_info`. Third, identify with `POST /api/v1/identification`, passing the key in an `Api-Key` header and a JSON body of base64 `images`. Send Malwa `latitude`/`longitude` and the photo `datetime` every time: the spec says location and date can improve accuracy and help with seasonal differences ([crop.health OpenAPI](https://crop.kindwise.com/api/v1/openapi.yaml)). Read `result.disease.suggestions[]` and use the `probability` field. The `details` parameter can return `treatment`, `symptoms`, `severity`, `eppo_code` and `gbif_id`. Language goes in as up to three ISO codes, and **Hindi coverage is unverified**, so keep the app's own Hindi text keyed by class `id`. The Python client is `pip install kindwise-api-client`. The README warns that crop.health is **in beta and may change in incompatible ways** ([crop-health-examples](https://github.com/flowerchecker/crop-health-examples)).

**Budget.** Spend the 100 free credits on a probe of 5–10 field photos for each target before the demo. The returned `id`/`scientific_name` values answer the coverage table above. A field pilot needs paid credits: €50 buys 1,000 calls, and €300 buys 10,000 at €0.03. Kindwise offers discounted pricing to environmental NGOs and discounts for identifying fewer classes, via support@kindwise.com. **No hackathon or academic programme is advertised** ([Kindwise pricing](https://www.kindwise.com/pricing)). No latency figures or requests-per-second limits are published, so measure them during the trial. Plantix's Vision API covers 57 crops and has Indian partners such as DeHaat and AgroStar, but access starts with "book a demo", which rules it out for a one-day build ([Plantix Vision API](https://plantix.net/en/business/plantix-vision-api)).

## Open-Meteo extras and Sentinel-1 radar are the cheapest wins

The pest literature for Malwa's diseases depends on **moisture duration and lagged rainfall**, and the app reads neither yet. In central India (2017–2019), Rhizoctonia aerial blight advanced fastest at a **weekly mean Tmax of 27–30°C and RH of 80–90%** in the current week, after **higher rainfall and more rainy days in the previous week** ([IJBSM](https://ojs.pphouse.org/index.php/IJBSM/article/view/6028); [J. Agrometeorology](https://zendy.io/title/10.54386/jam.v23i1.90)). A second central-India model adds **dense canopy, 28–30°C and RH >85%** ([DOAJ record](https://openaccess.library.uitm.edu.my/find/Record/doaj-art-4e56c4251105426fa55fa070a38934d5)). Canopy closure is something the satellites can see. Waterlogged topsoil is something the soil-moisture fields can see.

Open-Meteo already serves these variables under the same no-key, non-commercial terms. It has hourly soil moisture at five depths (0–1 cm to 27–81 cm), soil temperature at 0/6/18/54 cm, actual ET, FAO-56 ET0 and **vapour pressure deficit**. Leaf wetness, however, exists **only as a daily mean leaf-wetness probability** ([Open-Meteo docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)). Hourly leaf-wetness duration therefore has to be derived from RH ≥ ~90%, dew-point depression and rain hours, which the app already pulls. The historical API adds **ERA5-Land at 0.1° from 1950** (about 5 days behind) for back-testing ([Open-Meteo historical](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)). The ensemble API, with 51 ECMWF members out to 15 days, makes probabilistic alerts such as "P(RH > 85% for 3+ days)" possible ([Open-Meteo ensemble](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/ensemble-api)). Two limits apply. The free tier is capped at **10,000 calls/day**, and **commercial use of the historical and ensemble APIs needs the Professional plan** ([Open-Meteo pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing)).

Sentinel-1 C-band radar solves the monsoon cloud problem that cripples Sentinel-2 during kharif. Planetary Computer serves a terrain-corrected `sentinel-1-rtc` collection with the **same anonymous STAC and SAS-token mechanism** the app already uses ([MPC SAS docs](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/docs/concepts/sas); [S1 RTC tutorial](https://e-marshall.github.io/cloud-open-source-geospatial-datacube-workflows/sentinel1/nbs/4_read_pc_data.html)). With Sentinel-1C and 1D in nominal operation since **24 June 2026**, the revisit is **6 days** ([ESA](https://collgs.esa.int/?p=8457); [CDSE](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/3949)). That gives roughly one or two looks a week. VH backscatter tracks canopy biomass and closure, and low VV after rain on flat SRTM cells flags waterlogging. Both map onto aerial blight preconditions. Two caveats are **unverified**: whether MPC has ingested the 1C/1D scenes over Malwa, and whether open issues #404/#417 on terrain-correction steps are fixed ([PlanetaryComputer issues](https://issues.ecosyste.ms/hosts/GitHub/repositories/microsoft%2FPlanetaryComputer/issues)). Check the latest item dates for the Malwa bounding box first. A free Copernicus Data Space account is the backup source, with **10,000 Sentinel Hub processing units a month** ([CDSE](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/node/2602)).

Observed rainfall corrects model rain during convective monsoon bursts. **GPM IMERG Early** is 0.1° and half-hourly, with **about 4 h latency**, and needs an instant NASA Earthdata login ([NASA GPM](https://gpm.nasa.gov/taxonomy/term/1357)). **IMD 0.25° gridded rainfall** (1901–present, real-time daily) needs no key through `imdlib`, but the imdpune.gov.in server is "sometimes unavailable", so cache it ([imdlib](https://imdlib.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Usage.html); [imdR](https://cran.r-universe.dev/imdR/doc/manual.html)). Preliminary CHIRPS has about a 2-day lag ([data.gov CHIRPS](https://catalog.data.gov/harvest_record/d89b70f7-b72d-4f4e-a9bf-44f4848243ef/raw)). **SMAP L4** root-zone soil moisture (9 km, 3-hourly, from 2015; Earthdata login) is assimilated rather than modelled, so it is useful for validating Open-Meteo soil moisture. Its latency was about **15 days in a 2015 analysis, and the current figure is unverified** ([NSIDC](https://nsidc.org/data/spl4smgp/versions/2); [JPL](https://smap.jpl.nasa.gov/system/internal_resources/details/original/324_116-Leon.pdf)). That makes it a training feature, not a live one.

Several sources add little or cannot be reached quickly. NASA POWER needs no key but is ~50 km and mostly duplicates Open-Meteo ([POWER methodology](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/)). ESA CCI soil moisture ends on **31 Dec 2024**, so it is useful for climatology only ([ESA CCI](https://climate.esa.int/en/catalogue/2600f842cfeb481bb21f032a5741c353/)). The official IMD API requires profile validation and an organisational nodal officer, though IMD says APIs are free for non-commercial use ([IMD API](https://api.imd.gov.in/); [IMD RTI](https://internal.imd.gov.in/section/rti/rticases/20260112_rti_211.pdf)). MOSDAC's SMAP-derived Soil Wetness Index is open access, but other data needs an approved account ([MOSDAC](https://mosdac.gov.in/node/2070); [MOSDAC access](https://mosdac.gov.in/node/765)). VEDAS/Krishi-DSS publish block- and district-level soil moisture and NDVI with **no documented public API** ([ISRO VEDAS](https://www.isro.gov.in/VedasServices.html)). Google Earth Engine's free Community tier gives **150 EECU-hours a month** for verified non-commercial projects ([GEE tiers](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/noncommercial_tiers)). It is not needed, because MPC already covers the imagery. **Planet NICFI is gone**: Global Forest Watch removed the imagery on 1 April 2025 ([GFW](https://www.globalforestwatch.org/blog/data-and-tools/planet-imagery-changes-gfw)).

## KCC data is a weak, lagged label, and hard to download

The AIKosh entry "Kisan Call Centre – Transcripts of farmers queries and answers" is only a **redirect stub**. It shows size 0, year range "N.A.", and no API. It points to the data.gov.in resource under the Open Government Licence, so there is no reason to log into AIKosh ([AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/datasets/details/kisan_call_centre_kcc_transcripts_of_farmers_queries_and_answers.html)). Search snippets of the data.gov.in page confirm that it has StateName and DistrictName fields. The same snippets say **the API "does not exist"** for this resource and offer a "Request API" button (ndsap@gov.in). This could not be checked first-hand, because the site returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches ([data.gov.in resource](https://www.data.gov.in/resource/kisan-call-centre-kcc-transcripts-farmers-queries-answers)).

The working path, **all unverified and to be tested in this order**, has four steps. First, register on data.gov.in, activate the account from the email link, and copy the key from "My Account" ([datagovindia README](https://ftp.eenet.ee/pub/cran/web/packages/datagovindia/readme/README.html)). Second, try `GET https://api.data.gov.in/resource/cef25fe2-9231-4128-8aec-2c948fedd43f?api-key=KEY&format=json&limit=1`. That UUID is the one the Hugging Face KCC-Pairs mirror cites as its source ([HF KCC-Pairs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/judicialmind/KCC-Pairs)). Third, if the call works, filter `filters[StateName]=MADHYA PRADESH` and page with `offset`. Historic figures put the cap at about 100 rows per call, but those figures are old ([R-bloggers 2014](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2014/04/accessing-open-data-portal-india-using-apis/)). Fourth, if it fails, download the CSV from the resource page. Do the crop spelling (Soybean/Soyabean), the "Plant Protection" query type, and fuzzy pest keywords (girdle/gardle, safed makkhi, semi looper) locally, and read the API `field` array before hard-coding any column names. Two mirrors are worth a look for the surge-calendar script. **judicialmind/BharatDistrictPanel** has KCC category counts for 2009–2025, with cells under 10 suppressed, which could give monthly volumes by district directly. **KCC-Pairs** has about 5M de-identified Q&A pairs, is gated, and probably lacks dates. Neither card's schema could be fetched ([BharatDistrictPanel](https://huggingface.co/datasets/judicialmind/BharatDistrictPanel)).

The authoritative archive is ICAR-IASRI's **KCC-CHAKSHU**, which holds over 40 million preprocessed queries. ICAR studies use it with data running to at least **May 2024**, and its records carry the call date, district, crop and query nature ([IJEE Haryana](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJEE/article/view/156241); [IJEE Rajasthan](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJEE/article/view/146208)). It has no public export, and the server refused connections from this environment, so bulk data means a request to IASRI. Calls lag symptoms rather than predicting them, so KCC is best used as **a seasonal prior and a validation label**. The team's hypothesis that MP soybean pest calls peak in **standard meteorological weeks (SMW) 30–38** is untested. No published KCC peak data for soybean by week or district was found, and the one soybean-specific KCC paper (Godara et al. 2024) was available only as an abstract ([KRISHI record](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/940744)). Field data from Rajasthan supports that window: whitefly and jassid started in the **2nd week of August** and peaked from **late August to September**, and *S. litura* peaked in the **4th week of September** ([kiran.nic.in](https://www.kiran.nic.in/pdf/IJHF/Vol_31_1/9.pdf)).

## Ground-truth surveillance exists, but only behind MoUs

There is no open, machine-readable feed of pest incidence for MP soybean. The best potential ground truth is **NPSS**, launched by MoA&FW on 15 Aug 2024 and run by DPPQS and ICAR-NCIPM. It collects geotagged farmer photos for 61 crops. **No API or open download was found** ([Vikaspedia](https://agriculture.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/agriculture/ict-applications-in-agriculture/national-pest-surveillance-system-ict-applications-in-agriculture-3?lgn=en); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fagriculture-ministry-launches-national-app-to-collect-info-on-pest-attacks-in-crops-3153384)). **CROPSAP** has collected village-level soybean scout data in Maharashtra since 2009-10. It began after a Spodoptera outbreak, and it has already powered a girdle beetle forewarning model for 20 districts that explained **74–95% of variation by zone** ([StudyIQ](https://studyiq.com/articles/crop-pest-surveillance-system-and-advisory-project); [J. Agromet mirror](https://typeset.io/pdf/gis-based-pest-weather-model-to-predict-the-incidence-of-3dzmhm919p.pdf)). Both need requests to NCIPM or the state department. ICAR-IISR Indore's weekly bilingual advisories, archived on KRISHI, are a same-day alternative. Parse them into "expert alert weeks" to use as weak positive labels for Malwa ([KRISHI advisory PDF](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/bitstream/123456789/23316/1/WeeklyAdvisory%287%20to%2013%20Oct%202019%29.pdf)).

Citizen science helps only for conspicuous species. The live iNaturalist API (no key) returns **880 research-grade *S. litura* records in India, but zero for girdle beetle** ([iNat species counts](https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations/species_counts?place_id=6681&quality_grade=research&taxon_id=47157&q=Spodoptera); [iNat girdle beetle](https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations?taxon_name=Obereopsis%20brevis&place_id=6681&quality_grade=research&per_page=1)). Most research-grade records already flow into the app's cached GBIF data, so iNaturalist adds freshness and unverified "needs ID" records. It does not add incidence. EPPO's REST API (free registration and token) supplies host ranges and codes that match crop.health's `eppo_code` field ([CRAN pestr](https://stat.ethz.ch/CRAN/web/packages/pestr/refman/pestr.html)). Host density and soil context come from **data.gov.in "District wise Soyabean Farming Area in MP"** ([data.gov.in](https://www.data.gov.in/catalog/district-wise-soyabean-farming-area-madhya-pradesh)) and **ICRISAT's District Level Database**, which covers 1966–2017 ([KRISHI](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/ohs-2.3.1/index.php/record/view/662239)). Soil Health Card village-level nutrient data is available through the India Data Portal mirror, updated Aug 2025 ([India Data Portal](https://ckandev.indiadataportal.com/dataset/soil-health-card/resource/024cf507-4281-4c89-a40e-37b5add3a4df)). SoilGrids 250 m is probably open but is **unverified** this session. UPAg has **no documented public API** ([Parse.bot](https://parse.bot/marketplace/07fa78ed-8d38-4eeb-a4dd-9f3482166be4/upag-gov-in-api)). Agmarknet daily mandi prices via data.gov.in turn risk into spray-or-not economics, but one developer says the portal is "frequently down / WAF-blocked" ([Agmarknet MCP](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/Krishna-Baldwa/agmarket-mcp)).

There are three gaps. No numeric thresholds were retrieved for **soybean rust, anthracnose or semilooper**, and per-pest economic threshold levels from the IISR package of practices were also not retrieved. Rust and anthracnose rules therefore have to rest on the leaf-wetness proxy until a source is found. Sehore (Malwa-adjacent) weekly girdle beetle tables from 2015 exist and would be the closest local check ([J. Pharmacognosy & Phytochemistry](https://www.phytojournal.com/archives/2019/vol8issue4/PartAW/8-4-590-649.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go. The photo API is the most visible addition but the least decisive one. Its independent accuracy is about 48% top-1, it lacks proof of the two most Malwa-specific problems (YMV and girdle beetle), and its pest classes stop at family level. Its value is as one vote in the evidence fusion that already exists. The prediction model, by contrast, can improve substantially in a day without a single new key. The aerial blight and YMV rules depend on moisture persistence, monsoon breaks and canopy closure, and the app can now measure all three through Open-Meteo extras, observed rainfall and cloud-proof radar.

The structural constraint is labels, not features. Every source that records where and when pests actually appeared (NPSS, CROPSAP, CHAKSHU) sits behind an institutional request, and KCC is a lagged echo of those events rather than a measurement. A pitch that frames FasalNetra as "rules from MP literature now, recalibrated on NPSS/CROPSAP data under an NCIPM data-sharing request" is both honest and fundable. Separately, the free tiers of Open-Meteo, IMD and GEE are all non-commercial, so any paid deployment has a licensing step before it has a scaling step.
